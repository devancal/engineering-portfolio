Same stroke. Different motion.

The 40 mm crank radius sets an 80 mm stroke. As the crank turns, the rod changes angle, so piston velocity varies even at constant crankshaft speed. The finite rod length shifts peak velocity to about 81.1° and 278.9°, rather than exactly a quarter-turn.

Twice the RPM. Four times the acceleration.

At 3,000 RPM, peak speed is 12.73 m/s and peak acceleration magnitude is 4,579.50 m/s². At 6,000 RPM they become 25.45 m/s and 18,317.99 m/s². Geometry is unchanged; velocity scales with angular speed, and acceleration with its square.

ENGINEERING INSIGHT / SCOPE

This connected assembly geometry to measurable motion and showed why increasing RPM has a much stronger effect on acceleration than on velocity. It is a kinematic analysis of one ideal, rigid crank-slider at constant speed, not a full engine simulation. Combustion, thermodynamics, cylinder pressure, stress/FEA, heat transfer, bearing loads, friction and complete engine dynamics are outside its scope.
