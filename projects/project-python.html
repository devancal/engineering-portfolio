V8 Crank-Slider Kinematics Analysis

A Python extension of my V8 CAD work using the engine’s 40 mm crank radius and 250 mm connecting-rod geometry to calculate and visualize piston motion through a complete crankshaft revolution.

CONTEXT
Personal project
TOOLS
Python · NumPy · Matplotlib
FOCUS
Crank-slider kinematics

Goal & challenge

Goal. Connect the geometry of my V8 CAD model to engineering computation by translating the crank-slider mechanism into a mathematical model of piston displacement, velocity, and acceleration.

Geometry. The model uses a 40 mm crank radius and 250 mm connecting rod from the CAD geometry, producing an 80 mm stroke and a connecting-rod/crank ratio of 6.25. I evaluated the primary case at an assumed 3,000 RPM analysis speed rather than treating that speed as a tested operating limit of the engine.

# piston displacement from TDC
A = L**2 - r**2 * np.sin(theta)**2
x = r*np.cos(theta) + np.sqrt(A)
displacement = (r + L) - x
# crank speed → angular velocity
omega = rpm * 2*np.pi / 60

Approach & engineering work

  • 3,000 RPM: Calculated a peak piston speed of 12.73 m/s and peak kinematic acceleration magnitude of 4,579.50 m/s².
  • Geometry effect: Peak positive piston velocity occurs at 81.1° and peak negative velocity at 278.9°, showing the departure from simple harmonic motion caused by finite connecting-rod length.
  • RPM study: Compared 1,000, 3,000, and 6,000 RPM. Peak speed increased from 4.24 to 25.45 m/s, while peak acceleration increased from 508.83 to 18,317.99 m/s².
  • Visualization: Built displacement, velocity, and acceleration plots plus an animated crank, connecting rod, and reciprocating piston with live crank angle and displacement.

ITERATION / TAKEAWAY

For fixed geometry, the results demonstrate the expected linear relationship between crankshaft speed and peak piston velocity and the quadratic relationship between crankshaft speed and peak piston acceleration. Doubling speed from 3,000 to 6,000 RPM approximately doubled peak velocity while quadrupling peak acceleration. The analysis is kinematic only; component forces, combustion pressure, stress, friction, and material behavior are outside its scope.
