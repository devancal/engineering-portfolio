V8 Engine Internal Combustion Design

My first large engine CAD project: a multi-component V8 assembly built in Onshape to understand how dimensions, fit, piston motion, and cam–valve relationships interact inside a complete mechanism.

Goal & challenge

Goal. I chose a V8 because cars and engines are a major personal interest, and I wanted to move beyond isolated parts into a complete engine assembly. Before this, the water-pump project was my main assembly experience, so the V8 was a substantial step up in part count, fit, and mechanical interaction.

Challenge. Dimensional accuracy became one of the biggest constraints. Small mistakes that looked harmless while modeling individual parts became much harder to correct once those parts reached the full assembly.

Approach & engineering work

  • Approach: Built a parametric, multi-component V8 assembly in Onshape and modeled the piston subassemblies around their mechanical relationships.
  • Fit & motion: Checked component interaction as the assembly came together rather than treating each part as an isolated model.
  • Motion constraint: Explored cam-to-valve motion using tangent mates because Onshape did not provide a dedicated cam mate for the mechanism.
  • Iteration: Revised dimensions and geometry when interference or misalignment appeared during assembly.

ITERATION / TAKEAWAY

Tangent mates became difficult to manage across all 16 valves, which limited the cam–valve motion I could realistically build in the Onshape version. That limitation directly motivated the later SolidWorks rebuild, where dedicated cam and gear mates made a more complete driven mechanism possible. The project also changed how I approach CAD: I now put more emphasis on checking critical dimensions and interfaces before a mechanism becomes a completed assembly.
