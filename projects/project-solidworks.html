V8 Engine Motion Study

A second V8 built entirely in SolidWorks as a deliberate progression from the Onshape project—broadening my CAD proficiency and using cam, gear, and motion tools that allowed a more complete driven mechanism.

Goal & challenge

Goal. I rebuilt the V8 in SolidWorks to become comfortable in another major CAD platform and to revisit the same mechanical system with a richer set of assembly and motion tools. Because I already understood the engine layout from the Onshape project, I could focus more directly on modeling decisions, mates, and motion.

Challenge. I modeled every component myself. The crankshaft required multiple extrusions while preserving the geometry needed to align with the connecting rods, and the intake-manifold openings had to line up correctly with the cylinder heads.

Approach & engineering work

  • Assembly: Modeled the complete engine from individual SolidWorks parts and assembled them with coincident, concentric, width, cam, and gear mates.
  • Progression: Used SolidWorks cam mates to build the cam-and-valve relationship that was difficult to achieve in the earlier Onshape version.
  • Motion: Created the Motion Study by driving the crank gear as the motor input and using the assembly mates to transmit motion through the mechanism.
  • Validation: Used the assembled mechanism and its motion relationships to expose fit and geometry issues that were not obvious at the individual-part level.

ITERATION / TAKEAWAY

Assembly checks exposed two concrete geometry problems. An exhaust mounting panel extended too far and overlapped nearby geometry, while one crankshaft extrusion was too short to align with a piston connecting rod. I returned to the source parts, corrected the dimensions, and rechecked the assembly. Those fixes are a better representation of the project than a perfect first-pass model: the assembly itself became a tool for finding design errors.
